# Zariia — the name, the form, the carrier

*Recorded 25 July 2026, rescoped 26 July. This is the project's identity and its form: a record
that carries a sealed second channel, unlocked by a key.*

---

## What Zariia is

**A label / venue / platform that releases music with hidden sound channels, unlocked by a key.**

The named borrowing is deliberate. Zariia is a mutation of ***Zaireeka***, the Flaming Lips album
of 28 October 1997: four CDs meant to play at once on four stereo systems, each disc partial, the
whole only present when all four sound together. It grew out of Wayne Coyne's *Parking Lot* and
*Boombox Experiments* — dozens of cars or boomboxes, each carrying one part, a symphony assembled
from many machines. That album's name welds **Zaire** (chosen for anarchy, after a radio report on
the country's collapse) to **Eureka** ("I have found it"). Chaos joined to discovery.

**And said the ordinary Indian way, the name lands on ज़रिया — *zariya*: means, way, agency, the
thing something travels by.** From Persian *zarī'a*, from Arabic *ḏarīʕa*, and in daily use as
*ke zariye*, "through / by means of". The word names a carrier, which is what a Zariia object is:
a song a message goes by means of. The name is therefore legible in the language of the place it comes from,
and merely odd elsewhere.

The root holds a second sense, and it is the one that names the scene rather than the carrier:
**pretext**, in the plain meaning of *occasion* — the reason you can give for turning up. Nobody
joins a community; people turn up for a record, and the scene accretes around the occasion. The
music is that occasion. You come for the song, find there is more of it than you can hear, and
discover the undercurrent and the people who hold the key. So *zariya* names both halves — the means by which something travels,
and the occasion that gets people into the room where it travels.

The full discourse on the name — the Zaire syllable and the discomfort in borrowing it a second
time, the *zariya* landing, and the pretext as the form a subculture actually takes — is in
`the-name.md`. It is written to be said aloud when someone asks, because a project whose doctrine
is that it must be able to say plainly what it is cannot have a name whose history it would rather
not explain.

This project takes that structure and folds the extra discs *into the first one*, sealed. One track,
released openly. A second channel, locked. The key is what completes the work.

## 🔴 Zariia notates a structure that already exists. It does not invent one (30 July 2026)

*Prayas: "this second channel is already there. There are private parties, the scenes to which
you're invited only if on a person-to-person way. So this is just making it explicit. It's already
there — we're not saying that we are creating this, no, it's already there. But explicitly
specifying it."*

**This is the project's foundational claim and it governs how everything else is written.** The
two-channel structure — one thing said in the open, another said only to whoever is in the room — is
not a format Zariia thought of. It is how private gatherings have always worked. Zariia's
contribution is to make it explicit and to build a carrier for it, which is notation rather than
invention.

`curatorial-brief.md` §II.A makes a weaker version of this about *form*: the baithak has been the
invited room for two centuries, so a label that convenes rooms is not making an avant-garde gesture.
The claim here is stronger and about the present. The second channel already runs, socially, now.

Three consequences, and each one changes an argument recorded elsewhere.

**It defuses §V.1, the risk that the concept eats the music.** A notation of an existing practice is
not a conceit imposed on musicians. The format is not asking anyone to illustrate an idea; it is
writing down what the room was already doing.

**It answers `curation-by-demonstration.md` §IV.3 without claiming exteriority.** Bourdieu's finding
is that refusing publicity is a legible position-taking move *inside* the market rather than an exit
from it. That finding bites on a project claiming to have founded something outside the field. It
does not bite the same way on a project declining the premise that anything is being founded.

**It changes what the first release has to prove.** A label that invented a form has to justify the
form. A label that notated one only has to be accurate. The burden moves from originality to
fidelity, which is easier to carry honestly and harder to fake.

**How to say it, and how not to.** *This already exists; we are specifying it* is the register. *We
have made something new* is not, and neither is any phrasing in which Zariia is the origin of the
practice rather than a notation of it.

## The record of the form (25 July 2026)

The passage below is the concept as it arrived in conversation. It is kept verbatim because the form
was settled here.

> The experience: the released track plays for anyone and sounds whole. Hold the key and a second
> channel unlocks — more instrumentation, a counter-voice, a parallel layer — and it plays in sync
> with the open track. What you hear then is the complete work, the version as intended. Everyone
> gets a real song. Keyholders get the full one. Zariia's extra discs, folded into the first and
> locked.
>
> The mechanism is *encrypted*, not *buried*. This is not steganography — the second channel is not
> hidden inside the waveform so nobody can tell it is there. It is *locked*, not invisible. The
> second channel travels as an encrypted stream carried alongside the open track. An ordinary player
> plays the song and passes over the sealed part; the app, with the key, decrypts it and plays both
> together.
>
> That change gives back everything steganography was costing:
> - **No capacity ceiling.** The hidden channel can be full-fidelity, a complete second production,
>   because it isn't squeezed into the first — it rides beside it.
> - **No detectability problem.** The locked channel is plainly there in the file, and under this
>   framing that is correct: presence open, content keyed. The key protects the hearing, nothing
>   more, and that protection is real rather than a fragile act of concealment.
>
> If the second channel ever needed to be forensically invisible *as well as* locked, that would
> force it back to small and lo-fi — a ghost layer, not a full production. For the
> complete-experience-at-full-fidelity, the encrypted-alongside version is the honest fit, and the
> easier and more robust one.
>
> One consequence, stated plainly: a full-fidelity encrypted channel survives only where the exact
> file survives. Streaming re-encodes and strips it. That gives the object three states, and the
> three are a feature. Streamed, it is the open song — free, everywhere, the lure. Downloaded as a
> file, it carries the sealed channel. Downloaded plus the key, it is the complete experience. The
> shell travels the internet; the whole thing travels as a file and a secret.
>
> And here it closes. One key unlocks the complete work and membership in the scene that holds it —
> the same object, the same act. You decrypt to hear the full song;
> in holding the key you are already in.

### 🔴 Amendment, 27 July 2026 — the last three lines of that block are wrong

*The block above is kept verbatim because the form was settled in it. Its closing claim has since
been corrected by Prayas and does not survive: **"access to the sealed audio channel is not joining
the scene — being involved in the scene is."***

The key opens a record. It does not confer membership, and holding one does not put anybody in.
What the scene is made of is involvement — being in the room, playing, hosting, bringing somebody,
being asked back. The key is the **residue** of that involvement rather than the route into it: it
records a relation it did not create.

Handed over at the end of a session, a key is a trace of having been there. Passed onward, it is a
gift from somebody who was — which makes the recipient a listener who knows somebody, a real and
pleasant thing that is not membership. The chain does not manufacture members at each hop, and the
scene therefore stays the size of the rooms rather than growing with key circulation.

Where the old claim came from, and why it failed: `curatorial-brief.md` §II.B took Bellman's finding
on Kpelle Poro initiation — that entrustment makes a member regardless of what the secret contains —
and read the key handover as the same act. But Bellman's entrustment works because the Poro exists:
a society with standing, obligations, and the power to exclude. The ritual is the door of an
existing house. Zariia has deliberately built no house — `the-key.md` rejects the public-key model
precisely because a membership roster is the most dangerous object this project could make. Strip
the institution away and the gesture confers what it can actually confer, which is access to a file.

The consequence to accept honestly: the concept carries less weight, so the music carries more.

## The three states of a Zariia object

| State | What it is | Reach |
|---|---|---|
| **Streamed** | the open song, re-encoded | free, everywhere — the lure |
| **Downloaded file** | open song + sealed encrypted channel, bytes intact | passed hand to hand, byte-exact |
| **File + key** | the complete work | whoever was handed the key |

The public face is real music, and it streams anywhere because it *is* just music. The vessel is the
downloaded file, whose exact bytes carry the sealed channel. The key completes the record; it does
not enrol anyone.

## The law the form obeys (30 July 2026)

The relation this object is built on was written down years earlier, by the Museum of Vestigial
Desire. *Audience*, 8 October 2013:

> "Intensity and access are inversely related, the more the access the less the intensity and the
> less the access the more the intensity. … Every ear on which our message is incident dulls our
> message."

A record with a sealed channel is that law made into one object. The open track sits at the
high-access end and loses intensity for it. At the other end the sealed channel gains that intensity
back, because fewer people reach it. Nothing between them is resolved. The object runs both settings
at once, and the key is what moves a listener from one to the other.

The same text refuses to read the law as an instruction to stay small: *"Narrowcasting or
broadcasting are available choices and are employed as required."* So the position is not against
reach; it is against reach as the default. The brief's instruction to decide open against keyed per
release is that choice and nothing more.

## Why this is a subculture, not cassette culture

Cassette culture gated the **object** — you got the tape by being in the scene, and once you held it
you heard everything. Access and comprehension were one act, and the membrane sat at distribution.

Zariia moves the membrane inward. The object travels completely freely; everyone can hold the
track, play it loud, pass it on. The gate sits at decryption. Possession is universal; hearing the
complete work is keyed. The closer ancestors are the cryptolects and double-voiced traditions —
Polari spoken openly, meaning nothing to the constable and everything to the person it was for; the
exoteric surface and esoteric core of a mystery text. None of these is hidden. Each is in plain air,
and what is gated is the reading.

The one thing cassette culture did that mattered — hand-to-hand transmission — does not vanish. It
relocates to the key. The music is free and everywhere; the *key* passes person to person.

What that transmission does **not** do is make belonging, and the difference is worth holding
precisely because it is easy to lose. Handing someone a key gives them the whole record and tells
them somebody thought of them. Belonging is made elsewhere — in rooms, over sessions, by being asked
back. Cassette culture is a weaker ancestor here than it first appears: the giving there ran
alongside a scene of gigs, shops and letters that did the actual work, and it is that scene, not the
posted tape, which the giving was evidence of.

## Honest boundaries (do not let these blur)

- **Cultural "under the radar" is not technical "undetectable."** One is a mode of circulation
  (scene-based, below mainstream notice, a form of belonging). The other would be a property of the
  carrier. Zariia's carrier is *not* covert — the sealed channel's presence is open by design.
  What is protected is the *content*, by the key. Do not describe Zariia as hiding from anyone.
- **This is not evasion, and must never be built or described as evasion.** The subcultural framing
  is avowable — you can say plainly what the object is. That is the whole point, and it is also what
  protects it: the moment it is pitched as defeating surveillance, that protection is gone.
- **The content is only ever as safe as the key and who holds it.** The music sounding identical is
  solved. The confidentiality rests entirely on key handling. There is nothing else load-bearing to
  be fragile about, and nothing else to over-claim.
- **Full-fidelity sealed channels require byte-exact distribution.** Streaming strips them. The
  streamed song is the public shell; the complete object lives only in the downloaded file. This is
  a constraint to design around, not a bug to fix.

## Open

- **Is Zariia the whole project's name, or the carrier layer's name?** Recorded 25 July 2026 as the
  **whole project** — the repository is `zariia`, and the identity is the label/venue/platform.
  Zariia is a label; the sealed channel is its format.
- **Key model for a subculture — ANALYSED 26 July 2026, recommendation in `the-key.md`; Prayas
  decides.** Recommended: **per-drop symmetric keys, passed by people, carrying no identity.** A
  single scene key fails on revocation (one leak opens the whole back catalogue, permanently) and
  is not a secret once a few hundred people hold it. **A public-key scheme is a trap and should be
  rejected explicitly**: it buys tidy admission and revocation by creating a list of who is in the
  scene, which is a social graph by another name and the most dangerous artefact this project
  could hold. Per-drop keys need no directory and no server, and the fact that distribution repeats
  every release is the feature — a scene is maintained by continuing to be handed things, not by a
  one-time initiation.

  **The sessions are the distribution event**: if releases come from rooms people were invited to,
  the people in that room are the first keyholders and the first hop needs no mechanism at all.

  Two engineering changes recommended there: **12 words rather than 24** (128 bits is past any
  brute-force horizon and is far more passable aloud — halving the length roughly doubles the
  chance the key travels), and **never ship a release keyed by a passphrase** (a released file is
  in the wild, so the attack is offline and unlimited; 600k PBKDF2 iterations slow each guess but
  a human-chosen passphrase is exhaustible).
- **✅ Real recordings, whose? — ANSWERED 26 July 2026.** **Zariia specialises in COMPILATIONS made
  from RECORDING SESSIONS.** Musicians and bands are invited, every now and then; what comes out of
  the room becomes the record. So a release is not one artist's album that Zariia distributes — it
  is an occasion several people turned up to, and the compilation is what that occasion left
  behind. The carrier is authored music, never generated filler.

  Three things follow, and they are why this fits rather than being a house style picked at random.
  **It is the convening argument made operational** — the lineage in `historicising.md` is work that
  only exists while people are gathered, and a session is exactly that. **It gives the sealed
  channel something worth carrying**: the open track is one pass through the room, and the second
  channel can be another take, a counter-voice, or what else was sounding at the same time. **And
  being invited is the same act as being handed a key** — occasional by design, no release
  schedule, no submissions process.

  **Extended 30 July 2026 — records *and* residencies, and the residencies are for non-musicians.**
  Sessions remain the form a release takes. Alongside them Zariia runs **residencies: virtual,
  notional and unpaid** — a span of time and a frame, not a place or a stipend. A residency is
  preparation; a session is still a room. Terms and the reasoning are in `../residencies.md`.

  The residencies are **for people who are not musicians**, which gives the label the editorial
  criterion `curatorial-brief.md` §IV otherwise lacks. Not because trained musicians are lesser —
  positions are what get weighed here, never people — but because a training carries settled ideas
  about what performance and production are, and the odd work is likelier to come from a practice
  that never acquired them. The instruments in `../../music/` exist for exactly this: the firing,
  the stepwell and the zellij are sendable, and a residency can begin by handing one over.

  ✅ **Credit and pay settled 30 July 2026** (`../../open-questions.md` §2): a flat fee per person,
  the same for everyone in the room, paid before release; artists keep their rights and Zariia takes
  an exclusive licence to the session recording. What remains is the figure, which waits on funding
  rather than on a decision.
