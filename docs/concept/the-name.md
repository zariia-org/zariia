# Where the name comes from

*Drafted 26 July 2026. A discourse on the word itself, written to be said aloud when someone asks.
The form and the carrier are in `zariia.md`. This is only about the name.*

---

## The inheritance

Zariia is a mutation of *Zaireeka*, the Flaming Lips album released on 28 October 1997. Four
compact discs. Eight songs, each one split across the four, so that no disc holds a whole piece of
music. You are meant to play them at the same time on four separate stereo systems, started
together on a count.

The album grew out of something the band had already been doing outdoors. In the Parking Lot
Experiments they handed cassettes to people in cars and had them press play at once, dozens of
stereos in a garage forming a single work that existed nowhere except in the air of that garage. A
symphony assembled from machines that did not belong to the composer.

The title is a portmanteau. *Zaire*, which Wayne Coyne took from a radio news bulletin about the
country's political collapse and used as a word for anarchy, welded to *Eureka*, which is Greek for
*I have found it*. Chaos joined to discovery. That is the documented account and it is the one the
project has always carried.

## The syllable that outlived the country

Here is a fact that sits awkwardly, and I would rather set it down than tidy it away.

Zaire was a real place. Mobutu Sese Seko gave the Congo that name in 1971, as part of a programme of
stripping colonial nomenclature, and the name lasted until May 1997, when Kabila's forces took
Kinshasa and the country became the Democratic Republic of the Congo again. The album came out that
October. So *Zaireeka* was named after a country that had ceased to exist about five months
earlier, by an American band who had picked the word up off the radio because the news from there
sounded like disorder.

There is something uncomfortable in that, and the discomfort does not disappear at one remove. A
country's name became a mood. Then the mood became a title. Now the title has become a project's
name, and the project is a small label putting out records.

I do not think this makes the name unusable. I do think the honest position is that the first
syllable is borrowed twice over, that neither borrowing asked, and that anyone who traces it back
will arrive where I have just arrived. Zariia's whole doctrine is that it must be able to say plainly what it is. A name whose history you would rather not explain fails that test on its own
terms. So the history is written down here, at the front, rather than left for someone else to find.

There is also a resonance I want to name and then decline to lean on. A name for a state that was
erased, attached to a label about what survives being passed along. It is a resonance the project
inherited rather than earned, and treating it as meaningful would be exactly the kind of
retrospective tidying the paragraph above is trying to avoid.

The account does not end here, though. Two sections down there is a second origin, in the language
of the place this tool is for, and it changes what the name is doing without cancelling anything
written above.

## What the spelling did

Zaireeka became Zariia, and both halves of the source word gave something up.

*(Revised 26 July 2026. An earlier spelling, Zairiia, kept the diphthong; dropping it changed the
argument in this section, so the section was rewritten rather than patched.)*

The *eek* of Eureka softens to a pair of vowels and the hard consonant goes altogether, so the
discovery half is present only as sound, no longer as a word you could point at. And the first half
gives up more than it used to. *Zaire* has lost its diphthong. What survives is *Zar* — one flat
vowel, a syllable that no longer spells a country.

That matters more than a spelling preference. The borrowed name is now audible in the word rather
than legible in it, and the word has drifted away from the thing it was borrowed from and toward
the thing it means here. Zaireeka announces its construction; you can take it apart on first
hearing, and Coyne intended that. Zariia announces nothing, and is a short step from a word that
already exists.

A reading is available in the letters, and I mark it as a reading rather than a design. Zariia
holds two *i*s and no others, sitting together at the centre of the word: two identical strokes
side by side, one of which you only notice because the other is there. The object this project
makes is a track with a second channel running beside it, the same length, the same duration,
inaudible until someone gives you the key. I did not build the word to do that. It does it anyway,
and found resemblances are allowed to be pleasant as long as nobody starts presenting them as
reasons.

## The word it lands on

Say Zariia the ordinary Indian way and you arrive at **ज़रिया**, *zariya*.

The word is Hindi and Urdu, borrowed from Classical Persian *zarī'a* and behind that from Arabic
*ḏarīʕa*. Dictionaries give it as *means, way, agency, source, resource*; the Punjabi entry glosses
it as *way, method, source*. Its everyday form is the one everybody uses without thinking about it:
*ke zariye*, ज़रिये — **through, by means of, via**. Prayas's own gloss, a way out, a path, sits
inside that range.

Look at what the word actually names. Not the message and not the destination. It names the thing
the message travels by. A *zariya* is a carrier.

That is the project, described in one word of the language it is for. A song that a message goes
*by means of*. A room that a sentence crosses *by means of*. A key that a work is completed *by means
of*. When somebody in Ahmedabad or Srinagar reads the name and hears *zariya*, they are not making an
association. They are reading it correctly.

## The other sense: the pretext

The Arabic root carries a second meaning that travelled with the word into Persian and Urdu. Beside
*means*, *ḏarīʕa* holds *pretext* — and pretext in the plain sense, the occasion, the reason you can
give for turning up.

This is the better half of the word, and it names something the project needs and could not ask for
directly.

Nobody joins a community. People turn up for a record. Scenes are not founded, they accrete, and
what they accrete around is always an occasion — a night, a shop, a label, a song somebody made you
listen to. The occasion is doing the real work, and its usefulness depends on nobody treating it as
instrumental. A gathering that announces itself as a gathering repels the people it wants.

So the music is the pretext. You come for the song, which is a whole song and worth the trip on its
own terms. Having come, you find there is more of it than you could hear. Somebody has the key.
The discovery that an undercurrent exists, and that it can be entered, is the thing being offered,
and it cannot be handed over as a proposition. It has to be found.

That is what a subculture is, and it is why `zariia.md` locates the membrane at the reading rather
than at the object. Polari was spoken in the open. The pretext was a conversation; the undercurrent
was who understood it.

It also answers the bootstrap problem more honestly than any argument about preparedness does

So *zariya* names both halves at once: the means by which something travels, and the occasion that
gets people into the room where it travels. One word for the carrier and the excuse.

**And the honest limit.** Zariia is not spelled *zariya*, and it did not come from it. The word
arrived from an American band's portmanteau and landed near a Hindustani one. This is a found
resemblance like the three *i*s, and the same discipline applies.

Except that this one is not the same weight, and it would be false modesty to pretend otherwise. A
coincidence in the letters is an ornament. A name that means *the means by which something passes*,
in the language of the place it comes from, is doing work — it makes the
name sayable, explicable and locally legible in a way it was not an hour ago. It does not settle the
question in the section above about the borrowed first syllable. It does mean the name is no longer
only a borrowing. Part of it is at home.

## Saying it

The earlier spelling was close to unsayable. Za-ir-ee-a, Zy-ree-a, Zair-ee-ya — most people guessed,
and a good number guessed differently from each other. Zariia is not like that. It reads more or
less the way it is written, and for anyone with Hindi or Urdu it reads as a word they already own.

Something is lost in that, and it should be said. A name nobody can pronounce has to be told to
you by somebody who already knows, which is how the key travels and how the rest of this works.
The old spelling had that property by accident. The new one gives it up in exchange for being
sayable, and being sayable is almost certainly worth more — you cannot pass on by voice a name you
cannot say.

What survives is the asymmetry, and it is sharper now rather than weaker. To an Indian reader the
name is nearly transparent: it means *the means by which*. To a reader elsewhere it is a
pleasant-sounding word from somewhere, carrying no sense at all. The name is legible exactly where
the tool is meant to work.

I want to leave open the possibility that it suits this particular thing. Zariia's design already
turns on transmission between people: the key travels hand to hand, it arrives because somebody
thought of you, and the scene is a room with other people in it rather than a service you can
reach alone. A name you have to be told, by someone who already knows, behaves the same way. It
resists being picked up at a distance. Whether that is a property or a rationalisation of an
inconvenience is not something I can settle from inside it, and I would rather record the question
than answer it too quickly.

## What the name does not say

One thing about the name is unambiguous, and it was deliberate.

How a thing describes itself speaks to what it is for, and a name in the register of *ghost*, *shadow*,
*stealth* or *cloak* would be an exhibit before anyone examined a line of code. Zariia advertises
no capability. It does not describe evasion, or concealment, or defeating anybody. It is, in the
narrow sense that matters here, inert.

That inertness is not shyness. The project is meant to be avowable all the way down, and a name that
promises nothing is the first place that starts.

## What the ancestor actually gave

The borrowing is not decorative, and this is the part I would want said if only one thing were.

*Zaireeka* cannot be heard alone. Four discs, four machines, and in practice four people willing to
stand around and press play together, which is why so few people have heard it properly and why
those who have tend to describe the evening rather than the record. The work only exists when a
group convenes. Convening is the medium.

That is this project's structure exactly. Sound does not pass through walls, so a room is a natural
broadcast domain and the room is the network. There is no relay, no directory, no service to reach.
The work happens where people are together, or it does not happen.

And then the drift. Four consumer CD players started by hand never stay in step; they wander, and
the piece changes as they do, so no two playings are the same. For the album that wandering is a
gift, and people who love it love it partly for that.

For this project the same physics is a cost, itemised and unforgiving. Devices vary, rooms echo,
multipath smears the signal, and the published throughput figures are ceilings measured under
conditions no classroom provides. A systematic re-evaluation in 2025 looked at thirty-one acoustic
studies, found that not one had published its source code, re-implemented three, and after some
eleven thousand transmissions reported severe degradation from indoor multipath and device
variation.

So the inheritance runs the whole way through, including the awkward end of it. What was charming in
1997 is an engineering problem in 2026. The room gives you the network for free and then charges you
for it in bandwidth. That trade is the project, and the name was already carrying it before anyone
noticed.
